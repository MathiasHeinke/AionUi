# Art Direction

## Purpose

Turn a business goal and desired feeling into a visual system that belongs to
this company. The system must guide real design decisions, not merely list
adjectives.

## Visual thesis

Write one sentence in this form:

```text
The page should feel like [specific place or experience], expressed through
[composition], [materials or image world] and [motion behavior], so the visitor
understands [the page promise] before reading the details.
```

Bad: "modern, premium, innovative."

Better: "A sunlit civic workshop overlooking the sea, expressed through
architectural depth, fine bronze linework and restrained natural motion, so the
visitor feels invited to help build a credible future."

The example explains the method. Do not reuse its style for unrelated brands.

## Source of truth

Figma is not required. Any one of these can be the visual source of truth:

- an approved full-page design;
- a high-resolution hero composition plus a written system;
- screenshots of an existing page;
- a moodboard with explicit keep and avoid notes;
- an approved image-generation direction;
- a live reference page used for principles, not copied assets.

Record what is binding: crop, atmosphere, depth, geometry, palette roles,
typography roles, text placement and motion. Record what may change. A reference
is not permission to copy another brand's proprietary assets or exact identity.

## Direction workflow

1. Name the page's emotional destination and practical job.
2. List three visual clichés common to the category and exclude them.
3. Propose at least three concrete metaphors or places.
4. Choose the one that explains the promise in two seconds.
5. Define the system below.
6. Generate or sketch variants only when the choice is still unresolved.
7. Lock the selected route before implementation.

## System definition

### Composition

Define:

- dominant axis: centered, split, asymmetric, editorial or spatial;
- depth: flat, layered, architectural, photographic or diagrammatic;
- density: calm, balanced or information rich;
- focal order: what the eye sees first, second and third;
- negative space: where it is intentional and what it frames;
- section rhythm: expansive and intimate sections in a deliberate sequence.

The hero should read as one composition. Avoid placing unrelated panels around a
headline merely to fill space.

### Typography roles

Choose roles before exact families:

- Display: carries character and ambition.
- Reading: carries long copy without strain.
- Interface: carries labels, navigation and controls.

One family may cover several roles. Prefer self-hosted fonts when licensing
permits. Define responsive sizes, line heights, maximum line lengths and fallback
families. Do not use an ornamental display face for body copy.

### Color roles

Define color by function:

- canvas and alternate canvas;
- primary text and quiet text;
- structural line and surface;
- primary action;
- secondary accent;
- focus, success, warning and error.

Check contrast in actual combinations. A palette is not a collection of hex
codes; it is a hierarchy.

### Material and geometry

Define the treatment of borders, shadows, glass, texture, illustration, images,
radii, icons and ornament. Use one dominant material language. Glass is optional,
not a universal premium signal. Fine line drawings, motifs or texture can add
memory when they reinforce the brand and remain quiet behind the content.

### Image world

Specify subject, location, light, lens feeling, human presence, diversity,
wardrobe, architecture, season, color treatment and prohibited artifacts. If
people are not needed, do not add them for scale or decoration. If people are
used, their presence must support the story rather than become visual noise.

### Motion hierarchy

Use three levels:

1. Continuous ambience: water, foliage, light, grain or very slow drift.
2. Occasional events: a bird lands, a head turns, a shadow passes.
3. Rare signature event: one meaningful human gesture or scene change.

Motion should reveal hierarchy or life. It should not make every object compete.

## Anti-patterns

- Applying the same dark glass style to every company.
- Starting from a component library before deciding the place and feeling.
- Empty right columns, arbitrary floating cards or decorative dashboards.
- More than one competing visual metaphor in the hero.
- An image whose only job is to make the page look expensive.
- AI-generated text, logos, anatomy errors or inconsistent architecture in media.
- Copying a reference's brand identity instead of extracting its principles.
- Using people as repeated decorative units.
